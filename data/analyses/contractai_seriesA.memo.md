## Red flags
1. **Moat depth is partially self-reported.** The 4M clauses → 71%→88% accuracy curve is the load-bearing claim for defensibility. Verifiable in diligence (request the eval methodology and held-out test set), so this is an agenda item, not a gate.
2. **Foundation-model commoditization.** As frontier models improve at legal reasoning natively, the vertical fine-tune advantage may compress. Need to understand what part of the moat is data vs. workflow vs. distribution.
3. **Roadmap branching risk.** M&A diligence is a structurally harder problem than NDA/MSA review (multi-doc, cross-reference, deal-context). Spending Series A capital here could dilute focus from the working wedge.
4. **In-tenant data claim vs. cross-tenant learning.** "Customer-consented, in-tenant" is great for trust but raises the question of how labels actually compound across the network. Mechanism needs to be explained.
5. **Suppressed — "crowded market."** Harvey, Ironclad, EvenUp, Spellbook all play in adjacent space. Per our Zoom lesson, *"'Crowded market' is the laziest pass reason in our archive"* — execution differentiation (mid-market wedge, partner-verification UX, vertical depth) is the relevant question, not headcount in the category. Not raising this as a flag.

## Questions for the founder
1. Show the eval framework: held-out test set composition, partner-grader inter-rater agreement, and the accuracy curve broken out by clause type — does the 71%→88% lift hold on truly novel clause types or only on known-distribution ones?
2. Walk us through how labels from one tenant improve the model for another tenant given the in-tenant posture. Is there a federated step, or is it a base-model retrain on aggregated consented data?
3. What happens to your wedge if GPT-5/Claude-next can do senior-associate-quality contract review zero-shot? What's left that's yours?
4. Why expand contract types now rather than going deeper on workflow (partner verification, firm-wide knowledge management, precedent search) for the existing four types?
5. NDR 128% with 5-month office-to-firm-wide expansion — what's the gross retention, and what does the cohort curve look like for your earliest 10 firms?
6. Pricing: $33K average ACV against ~4 hours of associate time per contract feels like you're under-charging. What's the path to $100K+ ACV without losing the mid-market positioning?
