# ContractAI — Series A Fit Memo

## Fit summary
**Strong fit.** ContractAI sits squarely in two of our active buckets — vertical networks/marketplaces (specifically *law*, where we previously backed Casetext) and the 2024 "Defensible Niche Models" quadrant of our AI thesis. The deck's data-flywheel argument maps directly to our long-running view that "defensibility increasingly comes from network effects rather than technology alone," and the product clearly broadens access to legal capability for the mid-market firms our thesis cares about.

## Where it maps to our thesis
- **Vertical network in law.** Thesis 2.0: *"networks in high-value niches that are differentiated and defensible, partially because they are domain-specific."* Law is explicitly named; Casetext is the precedent.
- **Defensible Niche Models (2024).** Four Futures: *"data is more specific and targeted than in generalist models, so vertical players can defend a moat that horizontal ones can't."* Slide 6's clause-label flywheel (200K → 4M, 71% → 88% on edge cases) is exactly this argument.
- **Broaden access.** Thesis 3.0: *"Forge backs trusted brands that broaden access to knowledge, capital, and well-being."* Mid-market firms competing with Am Law 100 on speed = broadening access to a knowledge resource previously gated by headcount.
- **Data network effect, not just tech.** *"Defensibility increasingly comes from network effects rather than technology alone"* (Thesis 1.0, carried through 2024). Every accepted/rejected redline is a label — a classic less-obvious network effect.
- **Trust through alignment.** Thesis 3.0: *"Trust comes from true alignment."* The "partner verifies before sending" design choice is an alignment artifact, not a UX afterthought.

## Comparable past deals
- **Casetext (portfolio).** Same buyer (law firms), same wedge (associate-level work), explicitly cited as our law vertical bet. ContractAI is *more* AI-native and post-foundation-model, and targets contract review rather than research — a larger, more recurring workflow.
- **Sift Science (portfolio).** Vertical AI/data network where every customer's labels improve the shared model. Same flywheel logic; different vertical. ContractAI's data is more proprietary per-firm (in-tenant), which is both a moat and a constraint on cross-pollination.
- **Okta / SaaSure (passed, regret).** Founders from inside an incumbent (Salesforce → Okta; Coinbase GC + Cooley partner → ContractAI) building the obvious-in-hindsight workflow product. Lesson archive: *"founders who've seen the future from inside an incumbent are usually 18–24 months ahead… not the 5+ years we sometimes price in."*
- **Carta (portfolio).** Vertical SaaS into a professional-services-adjacent buyer that historically resisted software. Similar GTM challenge, similar expansion pattern (one office → firm-wide, mirrors NDR 128%).

## Red flags
1. **In-tenant data limits cross-customer learning.** Slide 6 implies a flywheel, but if data is contractually walled per firm, the "4M clauses" number may not be a single growing corpus. Worth probing — this is the load-bearing moat claim.
2. **Scope expansion in the ask.** $12M to add commercial leases *and* M&A diligence *and* build a new partner UI *and* move upmarket to Am Law 200 — that's three different bets. The deck targets mid-market (50–500 lawyers); the hiring plan targets Am Law 200. Which is it?
3. **Foundation-model commoditization risk in *Defensible Niche Models* quadrant.** If GPT-class models close the edge-case gap on contract reasoning, the vertical fine-tune advantage compresses. The 71%→88% curve is impressive but the absolute gap to a frontier model on the same task isn't shown.
4. **Buyer concentration in a consolidating segment.** 63 firms × $33K ACV = a TAM ceiling that needs the Am Law 200 expansion to work — which contradicts the mid-market positioning.
5. **Suppress: "crowded market."** There are visible competitors (Harvey, Ironclad, Spellbook, etc.) and our instinct will be to flag this. **Per the pass archive, "crowded market" is "the laziest pass reason in our archive" (Zoom).** Check for execution differentiation — the partner-verification UX and the mid-market focus look like real wedges — before invoking it.
6. **Suppress: valuation/round-size sticker shock.** If the $12M ask comes in at a price that feels rich on $2.1M ARR, recall Airbnb and Atlassian: 8% MoM with 128% NDR projects forward fast. Model the curve before reacting to the price.

## Questions for the founder
1. **On the moat:** Is the model trained on a single pooled corpus across customers, or are weights/labels siloed per tenant? If pooled, what's the consent mechanism and how durable is it as firms get more sophisticated about data leverage?
2. **On scope:** The deck says mid-market firms (50–500 lawyers) but the hiring plan targets Am Law 200. Which buyer is the Series A actually for, and what does the second one cost you in focus?
3. **On the foundation-model gap:** What's GPT-5 / Claude-class out-of-the-box accuracy on your edge-case benchmark today, and how has *that* gap moved over the same window your model went 71%→88%?
4. **On NDR 128%:** Is expansion driven by seat growth, contract-type growth, or price? The first is a network effect inside the firm; the third is not.
5. **On the team:** Maya is ex-Coinbase GC, Sarah is ex-Cooley partner — who owns the *mid-market* firm relationship? Cooley is Am Law 50. Is there anyone with a book in the 50–500-lawyer segment?
6. **On unit economics:** What's the inference cost per contract review at current accuracy, and how has gross margin trended as the model has gotten better?

## Recommendation
**Take the meeting.** This is on-thesis on at least three independent vectors (vertical network, Defensible Niche Models, broaden access), the team is credible, and the metrics warrant a real diligence conversation rather than a screen.
